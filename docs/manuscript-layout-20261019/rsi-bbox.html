Re-executing Scientific Design after Method Changes Working draft Public development manuscript skeleton, 19 October 2026 Abstract Changing a device, changing the method that designs it, and changing the procedure that improves that method invalidate different computations. We study these three interventions separately in an executable battery-free design apparatus. This development draft reports six deterministic invalidation controls and four fresh prompt-intervention attempts. Three delivered qualified computational designs; the fourth stopped at a frozen resource gate before validation. All 68 model requests settled. The method update remains unpromoted. No method advantage, recursive self-improvement, unseen-task transfer, hardware validation, or speedup is established. 1 Question and scope A previously successful device trace can cease to describe the system after a legal change. Reusing that trace may silently preserve obsolete control decisions. At the method level, a changed global design prompt may alter the first observation, the candidate, and every later experiment. Evaluating the old device under that prompt does not evaluate the changed method. Our question is which results remain valid after a typed intervention, and how to confirm a proposed method change without charging a cached response as a new design attempt. 2 Objects and validity contract Let D contain device structure, circuits, firmware, storage, communication, and deployment. Let H contain the design prompt, skills, available observations, context policy, experiment order, and organization. Let G propose and select changes to H. Device runtime, the agent’s decision span, and method generations are separate quantities. A record is reusable only when all consumed inputs, environment identities, numerical settings, and output hashes remain applicable. An evaluation change may reuse raw physical output while requiring a new verdict. For a global prompt change H → H ′ , our conservative rule restarts at task entry with a new owner, initial candidate, and context. Exact lower-level input responses may still be reused. A local continuation requires a separate proof that its restored state is valid. A G comparison additionally fixes the starting H 0 , development distribution, selection protocol, and improvement budget. 3 F1a: device-response controls We compare old fixed-trajectory replay, conservative dependency invalidation, boundary invalidation, full response execution, and exact-input response caching.
