\section{A/R: a battery-free entry to device and method changes}
\label{sec:phase1-ar}
The A/R case places an indoor-light-powered temperature and history tag on a
transport container. Its public apparatus couples assumed photovoltaic port
power, one capacitor, regulator thresholds, sensing and persistent writes,
program activity, and a digital receiving endpoint. Source power is specified
in watts; illuminance is metadata rather than a conversion to harvested power.
All runs start with zero stored energy. The source, ideal storage and write
models, and storage-side energy costs are uncalibrated computational assumptions.

\subsection{The retained reference and legal intervention}
We re-read the sealed reference without rerunning an unchanged design.
All 81 sealed files and 17 original positive and negative conditions passed
their original independent consumer. A phase-one consumer retains those
outcomes and adds explicit gate applicability and maximum inter-sample spacing.
The six-hour profile has 348 samples and its first fresh reception at 773~s.
In the twelve-hour dark history profile, storage retains all 170 records,
including 168 temperature samples across the whole profile. Yet 648 of the
720 historical dark-window bins contain no new sample. The recorded gap is
delivered, but absolute time after reset remains unknown; all 73 queued records
miss the recovery deadline and one of two required temperature events is missed.

The original gateway-recovery profile separates backlog completion from
timeliness: its 60 queued records are acknowledged within 82~s, and two repeated
arrivals cause no duplicate acceptance, but one new sample waits 30~s. Preserved
history, eventual upload, new sensing, and timely reception need separate evidence.

The frozen phase-one device space permits capacitance and the existing threshold
domain, with maximum voltage 3.3~V and all source and load assumptions fixed.
The participant can change sample, ACK and retry timing and supply a bounded
numeric program that ranks pending records. This program observes sequence,
record kind, and age only when synchronized local time and the record timestamp
support it. It cannot observe true creation time after clock loss, future supply,
or evaluator labels. Its expression is executed by the queue consumer;
submission alone cannot create an acceptance receipt. The frozen task also
requires the oldest unacknowledged record first. This ordering requirement
must be checked separately from the rank interface's syntactic legality.

\subsection{Conditional rank repair and the unresolved FIFO requirement}
The independent consumer passes energy, actual activity/program replay, CRC and
receiver checks for all twelve case executions. Under the rank interface, all
three candidates pass applicable computational service gates in the cold,
six-hour light/dark, and gateway recovery/deduplication profiles. In the gateway
case they retain 168 samples, acknowledge the 60-record backlog within 120~s,
and accept no duplicate from two repeated arrivals. Each of the 90 eligible new
online samples is received without an extra simulation-step delay. This changes
the fresh-delay gate from the original FAIL to PASS under the rank policy.

\textbf{This conditional timing repair is not compliant with the frozen FIFO
requirement.} Independent pending-record replay finds strict ordering violations
for all three candidates in both the history and gateway profiles. For the
submitted candidate, the gateway profile's first witness occurs at 833~s:
record~2 is selected while record~1 is still the oldest unacknowledged record.
The rank hook permits that choice, but the original task text does not.
The contract is unchanged and product acceptance remains \texttt{NOT\_ACCEPTED}.
A human must resolve this semantic conflict before any revised ordering contract
and its results can be accepted.

All three history profiles still fail continuous new sampling, dark new sampling,
120~s recovery upload and temperature-event coverage; absolute time after reset
remains UNKNOWN. Their whole-profile sample counts are 106, 168 and 129, not
counts of samples taken entirely within darkness. Faster retries reduce the
remaining late backlog while consuming energy that could support sensing.
The selected candidate retains 170 records, including 168 samples, and reports
the gap, but all 73 recovery backlog records are late and one of two events is
missed. Its fresh-delay gate passes only for the 78 eligible samples actually
created during online service; it does not fill the missing dark observations.

\subsection{A necessary bound and the D/H/G claim boundary}
Pi had access to Codex-provided necessary inequalities, including a stronger
sleep-plus-sensing dark bound. The final external proof independently uses the
weaker sleep-only bound below, without calling that analytical tool. Granting
all optimistic startup energy and omitting boot, sensing and transmission costs,
startup by 1,800~s at the legal ready-voltage infimum of 2.2~V requires
\[
C \leq \frac{2(90\cdot0.8-1)\,10^{-6}\cdot1800}{2.2^2}
  = 0.05281\ \mathrm{F}.
\]
In the fixed legal apparatus, the program cannot request power-off. Its fixed
ESR cannot cause a transmission dropout above the smallest off threshold, and
an ordinary brownout cannot recharge to a higher ready threshold in zero light.
Continuous dark sampling therefore needs power between its first and last
required samples. Grant full charge at dark entry and 122~s total edge slack
(one 60~s interval plus a 1~s step at each end). Even charging only 5~$\mu$W
sleep power requires
\[
C \geq \frac{(43200-122)\cdot5\,10^{-6}}
                   {(3.3^2-2.2^2)/2}
  = 0.07120\ \mathrm{F} > 0.05281\ \mathrm{F}.
\]
These disjoint necessary bounds establish a no-go only in the frozen ideal
single-capacitor, fixed-sleep legal space. They are not physical impossibility
or a participant discovery. Optimistic necessary limits suggest a cold deadline
of at least 2,426.93~s (40.45~min), a dark duration of at most 32,072~s (8.91~h),
or sleep power below about 3.7084~$\mu$W. None is a sufficient design. Any changed
service or apparatus needs a new human-approved contract and qualification.

The episode totals seven SETTLED provider requests, including the two from the
stopped segment. Their charge upper bound is USD~0.082605 and the cache-aware
rate estimate is USD~0.058482888; neither is an invoice. No provider usage is
unresolved at final readback. Codex apparatus, checker and repair assistance
remains \texttt{UNPRICED\_NONZERO}, so the full end-to-end cost is unknown.
The earlier four $H$ attempts, their 68 settled requests, all failures and all
historical UNKNOWN records remain separate and unchanged.

The actual episode supplies a device-level conditional rank repair and a
negative service result. A proposed $H$ rule could check joint startup/dark
feasibility and distinguish backlog completion from fresh delay, but no fresh
$H_0/H_1$ comparison or retained update is reported for A/R. A procedure that
proposes and selects that rule would change $G$ and need controlled lineages
and future tasks. Method benefit, recursive self-improvement and held-out
transfer are untested here; hardware qualification remains UNKNOWN.
