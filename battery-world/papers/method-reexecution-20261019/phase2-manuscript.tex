\begin{abstract}
A successful device does not by itself validate the method that designed it.
We report an actual model-authored method proposal in a frozen battery-free
apparatus. The original nine-request proposal episode failed at the host action
contract; a posthoc adapter recovered its rejected method text without another
model decision. Fresh baseline and revised-method agents subsequently produced
designs that both pass the complete computational service. Their first receptions
occur at 46.9 and 5.6 milliseconds, respectively. These observations support
distinct delivery strategies, but initial cache parity was not established and
the baseline contradicts the method's claimed necessary capacitance band.
The external decision rejects promotion and retains the experimental artifact.
Neither run establishes state retention across reset, method advantage,
heldout transfer, recursive self-improvement, or hardware qualification.
\end{abstract}
