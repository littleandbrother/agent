\begin{abstract}
Does proposing a design method improve battery-free service when proposal and
design share one budget? We compare a fixed method, ordinary reflection, and a
structured dependency and evidence-scope procedure on four fixed digital tasks,
with three formal repetitions per task. All three conditions pass 12 of 12
complete-service evaluations. They use 64, 117, and 127 provider requests,
respectively, including proposals and fresh design decisions. The registered
selection retains the unchanged base method. Dependency and scope controls
conservatively expand to complete feedback and establish no local-recomputation
speed benefit. Four formal groups select delivery across a reset, but all reuse
one historical ideal-model response; they are fresh Agent choices, not four
new physical experiments or a new memory mechanism. The proposed methods are
task-local instantiations. No global method transfer, change to the improver,
recursive self-improvement, or hardware qualification is established.
\end{abstract}
