\begin{abstract}
Battery-free design requires decisions about energy arrival, startup, event
capture, reset and delivery. We compare information and observation interfaces
on four fixed digital tasks with three formal repetitions per task and condition.
The average-energy arm achieves 8 PASS and 4 FAIL; complete-input, rich-static
and executable-response arms each achieve 12 PASS. Rich-static therefore remains
a successful strong control, and executable access shows no service advantage
on these tasks. Prospective live observation (E5) achieves 7 PASS and 5 INCOMPLETE,
with every incomplete group retained in the denominator. Its successes comprise
six selections of supplied fields and one constructed relation; none adds a
physical measurement. An equal-information JSON/table comparison achieves
12 PASS and 65 provider requests in each arm. Four fresh Agent groups select
delivery across a reset, but all reuse one historical digital response, rather
than constituting four new physical experiments. These finite public development
results establish operational interface behavior and its failures. They do not
establish interface superiority over complete static information, new physical
knowledge, hardware qualification, transfer or recursive self-improvement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and evidence scope}
An energy total alone does not specify when a device becomes ready, whether an
event is captured, whether pending state survives a reset, or whether a reply
reaches a receiver before its deadline. A richer interface might expose those
facts as supplied records, an executable response, or a model-proposed typed
observation. The relevant comparison must include complete static information:
otherwise a successful interactive arm could merely recover facts withheld
from its baseline.

This study asks whether richer or executable battery-free information improves
complete service under a matched finite budget. The strong version of the
registered hypothesis requires value beyond complete static inputs. The present
results do not support that stronger claim. The information ablation misses
service in four reset-task runs, while all strong static and executable arms
pass. Live observation demonstrates seven prospective source-to-consumer
chains but also five admission failures. We report these as distinct outcomes,
without treating a missing final execution as a failed physical design.

The evidence is the completed Phase 3 public development matrix and its frozen
protocol, repeat decision and source-bound ordered-event receipts~\cite{tables,comparison,protocol,freeze,ordered}.
The four task identifiers denote four fixed inputs. Repetitions, method owners,
cached responses and selected witnesses do not create additional tasks. All
scientific conclusions concern the fixed ideal digital apparatus and one
preregistered synthetic DC derivative. Physical qualification remains UNKNOWN.
