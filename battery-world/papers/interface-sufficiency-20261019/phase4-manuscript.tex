\begin{abstract}
Battery-free event service requires powered observation and accurate encoded
event times as well as energy-feasible delivery. We report a closed digital
study with 73 logical cells and 77 actual attempts. On the original O0 development
task, the candidate and a fixed rich-state control choose the same one-second
program, share one response and pass eight events; a 60-second periodic control
matches none. Three paired descendant lineages then compare fixed H0, descendants
of fixed improver G0, and descendants of one model-proposed G1 procedure.
All 30 effective selection cells pass before six methods are frozen. Across
27 final cells, H0 has 7 PASS, 1 submitted FAIL and 1 INCOMPLETE; G0 has 6/1/2;
G1 has 8/0/1. Both reserved digital tasks pass in all 18 cells. The new O waveform
separates event matching from service: both one-second submissions match eight
events and pass energy checks but fail encoded-time tolerances, while three
0.75-second submissions pass. Four token-censored episodes retain their costs
and denominators. The 8/9 G1 result is descriptive evidence conditional on one
shared formation event, not stable recursive improvement or a unique mechanism;
H0 also succeeds at 0.75 s. Physical qualification remains UNKNOWN and prior
A/R product failures remain unaccepted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and evidence scope}
A battery-free event application must associate a change in external activity
with sufficient powered observation, retained state and timely reception.
Increasing the message count alone need not satisfy the required event semantics.
Likewise, a successful event predicate cannot establish continuous sensing or
discharge other reporting obligations. A transfer study therefore needs an
unchanged task contract, matched hardware, a strong fixed event-state control,
and separate records of the participant's judgment and the host's evaluation.

O0 first tests the extension of the existing A/R digital energy and receiver apparatus
to vibration-based start, active keepalive and stop service. The participant
receives the additional RMS source and declared sampling/wake costs rather
than supplying an expected-event table. The comparison comprises a free legal
candidate, an A-style periodic control and a fixed rich-state event control.
It asks whether the candidate meets the original O service and whether an
observed result exceeds the strong control. The closed development receipts
support event-service feasibility within the nominal model, with no advantage
over rich-state~\cite{o0,first}.

The subsequent comparison asks whether a procedure that generates design
methods transfers beyond its selection inputs. H denotes the procedure used
by a fresh design episode; G denotes the procedure that proposes H descendants.
We compare fixed H0, the descendants of fixed G0, and the descendants of one
actually proposed G1. Six selected methods are frozen before any final input
is delivered. Each consuming final episode sees the complete frozen current
task facts, including its waveform. ``Unexposed'' therefore describes G/H
formation and selection, not concealment of the task from its final designer.

The evidence comprises the complete actual-cell ledger, final per-event details,
frozen selection and source-preservation records~\cite{actual,details,selection,preservation}.
The three descendant-lineage pairs are conditional on a single shared G1
formation event. They are not three independent G1 training runs. Similarly,
eight transitions on one O waveform are not eight tasks, and cached responses
are not repeated physical experiments. We retain submitted failures and
unsubmitted censored episodes as different outcomes and include both in all
reported denominators. H0 remains unchanged.
