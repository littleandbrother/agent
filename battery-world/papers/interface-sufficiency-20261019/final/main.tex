\begin{abstract}
Battery-free design decisions depend on when energy becomes usable, which state
survives a reset and what a receiver must learn. A representation that explains
one successful trajectory can omit the mechanism activated by the next legal
design change. We formalize this distinction as current-verdict versus
intervention sufficiency, give an elementary factorization condition, and
exhibit ideal storage-clamp and reset-memory counterexamples. We then implement
a bounded route from acquired source data through independently qualified,
typed observations to actual design consumers. A matched comparison uses four
fixed digital tasks and three formal repetitions per condition. Average-energy
inputs yield 8/12 complete-service passes; rich-static, complete-input,
executable-response and equal-information table inputs each yield 12/12.
Prospective live observation yields seven completed chains and five incomplete
groups, retaining all twelve in the denominator. Six completions select supplied
fields and one constructs a relation; none supplies a physical measurement.
A secondary event-service study separates event matching from timestamp
accuracy: two one-second designs match all eight events and pass energy yet
fail encoded-time bounds at 1.004 and 1.103 s. Three 0.75-second designs pass;
four other episodes terminate without a submission. These results identify
information and admission boundaries without demonstrating superiority over
complete static information. Physical qualification remains UNKNOWN, and prior
product failures remain unaccepted.
\end{abstract}
