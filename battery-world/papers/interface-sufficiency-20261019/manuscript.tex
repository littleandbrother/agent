\begin{abstract}
Battery-free nodes must acquire energy in time, store usable energy, start above rail thresholds and preserve events until reception. We distinguish a representation that judges a current service from one that supports legal design interventions. Two exposed structural pairs isolate storage saturation and reset retention; saved transient examples connect them to startup, ESR and deadline constraints. Rich static, complete raw and executable-model baselines remain available. A frozen input protocol and discovery-to-consumer contract prepare subsequent Agent experiments. Agent benefit, format benefit, physical qualification and autonomous observation discovery remain separate untested claims.
\end{abstract}
\section{Problem}
A fixed BF task $T$ and world $w$ define a service verdict $V(w,d,T)$. A representation $R(w,d)$ is current-verdict sufficient on a declared domain if equality of representations implies equality of current verdicts. For a public legal intervention set $\mathcal D$, intervention sufficiency requires the same implication after every jointly legal change $\delta\in\mathcal D$ is applied to both designs. The second property need not follow from the first. A passing present trajectory can leave an upper clamp or reset transition unexcited; changing capacitance or power schedule can activate it while the task stays fixed.

The BF question is whether the available description supports the chain from source timing and stored energy through startup, program state and receiving service under a proposed change. The structural witnesses isolate ideal energy-service and event-state guards; the separate transient reference checks receiver events against a deadline. Neither is a calibrated hardware model. This is a question about the information needed by a specific consumer: a static model containing the relevant state laws and parameters can already be intervention sufficient on its declared domain. Acquiring observations is useful only when missing information and its acquisition, qualification and use can be justified.

\subsection{E2--E3: two unexcited mechanisms}
Run \texttt{IF-7412532c4a6ab7a5879e} contains two public development pairs, not statistical replications or unseen tests. Each pair has identical current observed event/rail traces and two passing current tasks. The identical legal D change then yields FAIL/PASS.

For the clamp pair, a fixed 20 $\mu$W source supplies 40 $\mu$J before a 30 $\mu$J service transaction at 2.5 s, with a 1 V service reserve. Both systems use 10 $\mu$F initially. Their ideal clamps are 3 V and 5 V. The initial maximum rail voltage is $\sqrt{8}=2.828$ V, so neither clamp activates and the entire current trajectory coincides. Changing only capacitance to 4 $\mu$F is legal in the common $[4,12]$ $\mu$F domain. The 3 V clamp now spills 22 $\mu$J and makes only 16 $\mu$J available above reserve; it fails the fixed service. The 5 V clamp spills zero and delivers 30 $\mu$J; it passes. The post-change margins are $-14$ and $8$ $\mu$J. These are ideal energy transactions, not measured PMIC startup or MCU current.

For the reset pair, an event is captured at 1 s and must be transmitted once at 4 s, by a 5 s deadline. The external event and transmission time stay fixed. Without a power reset, volatile and retained pending memories both deliver the event. A common D change inserts an outage from 2 to 3 s: volatile pending memory is cleared and delivers zero events; retained pending memory delivers one. This changes a state transition rather than rescaling an old energy/window template.

The analytic clamp implementation agrees with projected time integration; the ordered memory machine agrees with a separate event-program parser and tick interpreter. Both 1 ms and 0.5 ms settings agree on all eight current/intervention cases. Maximum energy discrepancy is $2.84\times 10^{-12}$ $\mu$J. These implementations share ideal assumptions, so agreement is numerical/software evidence only.

Deleting the unexcited clamp threshold or reset-retention information restores aliasing in this encoding. It does not establish universal necessity of a field name or complete device-level E3 validation. Indeed an enriched \emph{static} model is sufficient on the declared ideal domain. It passes all 28 exposed legal-grid checks (20 clamp, eight memory), and a purely additive static control agrees with independent packet accounting on five service-cost values, including passing and failing designs. The clamp statistic is
\[
 M(C)=\min(E_0+P_{\mathrm{in}}t_{\mathrm{in}},CV_{\max}^2/2)-CV_{\min}^2/2-E_{\mathrm{service}}.
\]
Its sufficiency follows here from all input preceding service, an ideal cap, and no leakage or intervening workload. Those assumptions bound the claim.
