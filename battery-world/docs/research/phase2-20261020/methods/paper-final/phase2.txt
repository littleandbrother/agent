                             An Actual Method Proposal
                               for Battery-Free Design

                                                Working draft

                             Finite public development results, 20 October 2026




                                                  Abstract
     A successful device does not by itself validate the method that designed it. We report an actual
     model-authored method proposal in a frozen battery-free apparatus. The original nine-request
     proposal episode failed at the host action contract; a posthoc adapter recovered its rejected method
     text without another model decision. Fresh baseline and revised-method agents subsequently
     produced designs that both pass the complete computational service. Their first receptions occur
     at 46.9 and 5.6 milliseconds, respectively. These observations support distinct delivery strategies,
     but initial cache parity was not established and the baseline contradicts the method’s claimed
     necessary capacitance band. The external decision rejects promotion and retains the experimental
     artifact. Neither run establishes state retention across reset, method advantage, heldout transfer,
     recursive self-improvement, or hardware qualification.




1   Question, apparatus, and intervention boundary
This finite study asks whether an actual proposed design method should replace the baseline method. Device
changes D and design-method changes H have different validation scopes. Here D contains two device
parameters: capacitance and the earliest allowed transmit time. H contains instructions for observing sources,
forming energy and timing constraints, selecting a candidate, and checking it. A changed D requires its
complete feedback response; a global change to H requires fresh design decisions from task entry. Changing
the procedure that proposes methods, G, would require a separate lineage study. No such G comparison is
reported.
The fixed public development task is b-volatile-reset. One event at t = 0 must deliver exactly one valid
eight-byte application frame by 100 ms, without duplicates. Legal capacitance is 4–22 µF; the legal transmit
floor is 0–45 ms. Supply, initial state, event, all other device fields, and the evaluator remain fixed. Harvest
segments occur at 0–6 and 30–60 ms. The event latch is volatile. Passing means passing the complete
frozen computational service, including independent energy and receiver checks and transient-voltage and
deduplication checks. Physical calibration remains UNKNOWN; this is not a hardware or official benchmark
result.
A registered device-response negative control distinguishes replay from valid reuse: the retained 10 µF / 45
ms design fails, whereas 10 µF / 0 ms passes on the same world. Reusing the former response for the changed
input would give the wrong answer. The control read exact-input cached full responses and added no new
execution. It was prepared by the host and is not an observation generated by the method proposer. The
qualification-grid construction cost remains in the phase accounting.

2     Original failure and posthoc method recovery
The actual proposer used nine provider requests. Its live episode ended INCOMPLETE_OR_STOPPED: a host
action was wrongly exposed and a device check payload consumed a method-proposal slot. No original method
submission or adoption succeeded. A zero process exit code does not change that result. The host-action and
budget-contract failure, trace, and all nine requests remain recorded.
A posthoc host adapter recovered an actual model-authored method from the retained rejected call. It added
no paid proposal request or new model decision. The resulting artifact is eligible for experimental re-execution;
it is neither a researcher-authored replacement nor a successful original proposal episode. Its method-text
identity is bound as a canonical JSON string, with the hash encoding recorded in phase2-binding.json.
This recovery is explicitly posthoc and does not repair the original prospective protocol.
The proposed H instructs the agent to acquire all listed sources, construct an energy and timing ledger, avoid
losing the volatile latch before transmission, and check one selected design. It also makes device-specific
exclusions: it presents 7.4–14 µF as a feasible band, rejects waiting across the dark gap or using a late transmit
floor, and recommends 10 µF / 2 ms. These exclusions are scientific claims to test, rather than consequences
of passing the method schema. Its declared falsifier includes failure of the selected design and an edge-design
clause fixing a 2 ms floor and requiring selected-design failure. An outside-band success at a different floor
does not, by itself, satisfy that exact conjunctive clause.

2.1    Fresh entries and comparison limits
The baseline H0 and revised H ′ each ran from task entry with a distinct owner and context, zero initial decision
messages, and a limit of two candidates, 24 provider requests, and 1,200 seconds. Each actually checked and
submitted one candidate. The revised protocol appends the recovered method body verbatim to the common
instruction. Its actual choice, 9 µF / 3.7 ms, differs from the method’s 10 µF / 2 ms recommendation; this is
a fresh decision, not a literal copy of that device recommendation.
The external registry reports matching task, source, information, model configuration, tool, and evaluator
identities. Both use the provider-reported alias deepseek-flash; the weights are not independently pinned.
Crucially, the physical exact-input cache evolved and its initial snapshot was not frozen equally for the
two entries. The confirmation gate rejects with PARITY_INITIAL_CACHE_SHA256. Valid response reuse for
a particular input does not establish equal starting caches. These runs therefore provide descriptive case
outcomes, not a causal comparison of the methods.

The ordered-state audit resolves the reset interpretation. H0 receives at 46.9 ms and has no reset before
delivery or before the deadline. H ′ receives at 5.6 ms and resets at 12.1 ms, after delivery. Both have zero
resets before first delivery, and both event latches remain volatile. The baseline avoids a pre-delivery reset; the
revised design finishes delivery before reset. Neither successful run demonstrates that pending state survived
a reset.
The frozen checker’s legacy event_preserved_after_reset label is FAIL for H0 and PASS for H ′ . Its
implementation combines successful delivery with the occurrence of any reset; it does not test whether a
still pending event crossed a reset. We retain those labels while interpreting the actual temporal order.
In particular, the participant’s broad “no brownout” description is unsupported over the full deadline: the
post-delivery 12.1 ms reset remains in the audit. The checker was not changed.

3.1    Counterexample and retention decision
The passing 22 µF / 45 ms baseline is a counterexample to the proposed necessary 7.4–14 µF band and its
rejection of waiting through the gap or using a late floor. It is not a counterexample to the separate claim
that transmission must fit within one contiguous harvest segment: this baseline transmits during the second
segment. Nor does it test the proposal’s particular 22 µF / 2 ms edge case or the recommended 10 µF / 2 ms
design. The revised design’s success does not validate every rule in the method text.
The external decision is reject promotion; retain the experimental artifact. The default H is unchanged. This
decision preserves the original host failure and posthoc boundary, the already successful baseline, the missing
initial-cache parity, the cost of the proposal, and the over-restrictive method claims. The earlier reception
under H ′ is an observed outcome; with one case per condition and unequal cache provenance, it does not
establish a general or causal method advantage.

4.1    Evidence and reproducibility
phase2-binding.json binds the original failed proposal, recovered method, fresh-entry protocols and actual
tool traces, external service results, ordered-state audit, retention decision, and every cost ledger. It also
binds this manuscript, the shared style and official English university logo, and the resulting PDF. Source
hashes distinguish raw UTF-8 method text from its canonical JSON-string identity. render_phase2.py
build validates those inputs and rebuilds only this separate paper in two TeX passes; it does not execute the
scientific apparatus. Build and visual receipts are retained in docs/research/phase2-20261020/methods/
paper-final/.
All observations concern an exposed public development task under a fixed ideal model. No heldout transfer,
repeated-sampling estimate, independent method lineage, recursive self-improvement, hardware acceptance,
or overall product acceptance is established. The prior A/R fixed-space NO_GO, strict FIFO failure, and
NOT_ACCEPTED are preserved. The prior manuscript and PDF are preserved as separate artifacts.
