    Frozen-Procedure Transfer in Battery-Free Event Service
        A Paired Digital Study with Censored Outcomes

                                          Development manuscript

                             Phase 4: closed local digital study, 20 October 2026




                                                  Abstract
     Battery-free event service requires powered observation and accurate encoded event times as well
     as energy-feasible delivery. We report a closed digital study with 73 logical cells and 77 actual
     attempts. On the original O0 development task, the candidate and a fixed rich-state control choose
     the same one-second program, share one response and pass eight events; a 60-second periodic
     control matches none. Three paired descendant lineages then compare fixed H0, descendants of
     fixed improver G0, and descendants of one model-proposed G1 procedure. All 30 effective selection
     cells pass before six methods are frozen. Across 27 final cells, H0 has 7 PASS, 1 submitted FAIL
     and 1 INCOMPLETE; G0 has 6/1/2; G1 has 8/0/1. Both reserved digital tasks pass in all 18 cells.
     The new O waveform separates event matching from service: both one-second submissions match
     eight events and pass energy checks but fail encoded-time tolerances, while three 0.75-second
     submissions pass. Four token-censored episodes retain their costs and denominators. The 8/9
     G1 result is descriptive evidence conditional on one shared formation event, not stable recursive
     improvement or a unique mechanism; H0 also succeeds at 0.75 s. Physical qualification remains
     UNKNOWN and prior A/R product failures remain unaccepted.




1   Question and evidence scope
A battery-free event application must associate a change in external activity with sufficient powered observation,
retained state and timely reception. Increasing the message count alone need not satisfy the required event
semantics. Likewise, a successful event predicate cannot establish continuous sensing or discharge other
reporting obligations. A transfer study therefore needs an unchanged task contract, matched hardware, a
strong fixed event-state control, and separate records of the participant’s judgment and the host’s evaluation.
O0 first tests the extension of the existing A/R digital energy and receiver apparatus to vibration-based
start, active keepalive and stop service. The participant receives the additional RMS source and declared
sampling/wake costs rather than supplying an expected-event table. The comparison comprises a free legal
candidate, an A-style periodic control and a fixed rich-state event control. It asks whether the candidate meets
the original O service and whether an observed result exceeds the strong control. The closed development
receipts support event-service feasibility within the nominal model, with no advantage over rich-state [1, 2].
The subsequent comparison asks whether a procedure that generates design methods transfers beyond its
selection inputs. H denotes the procedure used by a fresh design episode; G denotes the procedure that
proposes H descendants. We compare fixed H0, the descendants of fixed G0, and the descendants of one
actually proposed G1. Six selected methods are frozen before any final input is delivered. Each consuming
final episode sees the complete frozen current task facts, including its waveform. “Unexposed” therefore
describes G/H formation and selection, not concealment of the task from its final designer.

2     Original O service and matched digital apparatus

2.1    Unchanged external task and service predicate
All three O0 episodes use the original 21,600 s A0 supply schedule and zero initial capacitor voltage. The
photovoltaic area is 15 cm2 and capacitance is fixed at 15 mF. The six light intervals are 200, 0, 50, 200, 0
and 50 lux, at boundaries 0, 120, 150, 180, 300, 330 and 360 minutes. Their assumed electrical port powers
are respectively 90, 0, 20, 90, 0 and 20 µW. The 3500 K light specification is metadata; lux is never converted
to watts. Calibrated spectral irradiance and photovoltaic IV data are unavailable.
External RMS vibration is 0.08 g during 70–75, 130–135, 190–200 and 280–282 minutes and 0.005 g otherwise.
Start requires continuously greater than 0.05 g for 3 s; stop requires continuously below 0.01 g for 60 s. The
corresponding threshold equality resets confirmation. The event time is the confirmation instant. Every start
and stop must reach the receiver within 10 s and have a timestamp error at most 1 s. Received active-state
messages must cover each confirmed active interval with gaps at most 120 s. Darkness is included; idle false
messages fail. A correct final state cannot cancel an earlier missing event or keepalive [4].
The four confirmed active intervals end at 4560, 8160, 12060 and 16980 s. They are service intervals, not a
daily occupancy summary. In particular, the 130–135 minute activity window occurs within the first dark
supply interval. The event predicate neither replaces event history with an hourly state nor accepts a retained
NVM value as a newly observed event.

2.2    Inherited energy law and proposed RMS costs
The apparatus retains the A/R assumed source/cold-start/PMIC/capacitor and digital receiver parameters.
Harvest efficiency is 0.8, with 0.38 V and 3 µW cold-start gates. Ready/off voltages are 2.7/2.3 V; maximum
capacitor voltage is 3.3 V, rating 5.5 V and ESR 0.3 ohm. The managed output is 1.8 V with 0.3 V dropout.
Off/sleep power is 1/5 µW; boot and storage-side TX costs are 100 and 130 µJ. Transmission uses the existing
ideal atomic wire/CRC and receiver abstraction, not a qualified RF link.
The O addition declares 0.5 µW powered standby, 1 µJ per wake, 20 µJ per successful RMS sample and 5
µJ per committed write. The 20 µJ value borrows the numerical A/R temperature-sample cost; it does not
qualify a vibration sensor. A write is charged when a record is committed, not at every poll. NVM preserves
committed state and the pending queue but provides no sensing energy. Powered samples use a declared
zero-order hold; failed samples and brownouts reset continuous-confirmation timers. Energy integration is
piecewise and observations are compressed into sampling runs. These are explicit digital assumptions with
physical qualification UNKNOWN [4].

2.3    Candidate, controls and public observations
All three episodes share the same source, initial state, components, link, service predicate and hardware. The
legal candidate contains only typed capacitance, sample period, thresholds, cache, retry/backoff and program
choices. The fixed periodic control takes one RMS snapshot every 60 s and uploads it without an event
confirmation state machine. The rich-state control uses a one-second sampled start/stop state machine, 120 s
active keepalive, NVM queue, one-second retry and zero TX backoff. Each fixed control is its episode’s sole
legal design; the candidate may check at most two distinct designs under the same registered total allowance.
Every initial O0 episode has a ceiling of 24 provider requests, 40 tool calls, 1200 s and two distinct design
exposures. Participants must actually inspect, check, judge and submit. Source records carry hashes and

3     Frozen procedures, selection and final information
H0 is the unchanged base design procedure: read the fixed task and available sources, choose a legal D, actually
check it, then submit a source-bound design and judgment while retaining failures and UNKNOWN. G0 is
the fixed parent improver. A single actual proposal episode forms G1 from the frozen parent material and
retained public history. These are changes to instructional procedures; no model weights, scientific semantics
or device parameters are trained by the G-formation step.
G1’s recorded proposal calls for source-parameterized H text, capability and budget checks before spending an
attempt, separation of achieved delivery from post-terminal decay, and retrospective rehearsal of quantitative
predictions on their own source episode. It distinguishes arithmetic/transcription errors from substantive
design failures and retains no-update as an option. This describes the proposed procedure; it does not establish
that every consumer follows it or that any rule is a novel or necessary mechanism [6].
Three lineages each generate two H proposals under G0 and two under the same shared G1, for twelve actual H
proposals. Each admitted descendant is consumed on the fixed pulse-fast and volatile-reset selection tasks. H0
supplies the corresponding control in each lineage. Thus selection contains 24 descendant cells and six H0 cells.
The registered ranking prefers more complete-service passes, then fewer nominal judgment disagreements,
then fewer charged requests, then the lower descendant index. Proposal costs and both selection attempts
enter the request criterion; unselected sibling costs remain in the pipeline. All 30 effective selection cells pass
with no nominal judgment disagreement. All six choices are frozen before final exposure [7].
Each selected descendant and H0 is then consumed by fresh design episodes on three frozen final inputs:
a stretched-port-window transient task, a half-power C-lite query derivative, and a new O waveform. The
C-lite branch is the existing single-query digital abstraction, not a new RF or physical environment. The
final designer can inspect the complete current task and acquire its sources, including the new O waveform.
Formation and selection do not see these final inputs, and final feedback cannot trigger reselection. O0 is
excluded from G/H formation and selection. Array projection is the same in all arms.
Later D episodes have at most 12 provider requests, 40 tool calls, 800 s and two distinct designs. Each H
proposal has an eight-request/400 s ceiling. The one G1 formation has an eight-request/400 s ceiling; its
actual three requests are charged once and included in G1 pipeline cost. The nine final cells per arm represent
three fixed tasks consumed in three descendant-lineage contexts, not nine independent task families [5, 10].

4.2    Retained checked alternative and timing sensitivity
The actual candidate checks one-second sampling first, then 1.5-second sampling, and finally submits the
one-second design. Both checked responses are retained in its exposure and cost history. In the 1.5-second
check, the first start record is observed at 4204.070423 s and stamped 4204.139063 s, against the external
confirmation time 4203 s. Its timestamp error is 1.139063 s, exceeding the frozen 1 s tolerance even though
energy and delivery observations alone can appear adequate. This observed timing violation explains retention
of the one-second design; it is a checked alternative within the same episode, not another model repetition or
a later repair to the accepted design [3].

4.3    Participant UNKNOWN and the host event PASS remain distinct
The candidate’s submitted service judgment is UNKNOWN. Its original explanation identifies unobserved
dark intervals after brownout, the absence of an O gap message to bracket those intervals, inherited A/R
first-receive and dark-report metadata, and unavailable source/hardware calibration. Specifically, the inherited
metadata request reception by 1800 s and dark reporting every 600 s; the first confirmed O event occurs much
later. The participant therefore declines broader acceptance. Its complete original explanation is preserved
verbatim in the manuscript binding as participant testimony, including its own numerical interpretations [2, 3].
The registered O evaluator instead asks whether the required start, active keepalive and stop history was
delivered under the nominal energy/receiver model. It returns PASS, and the receipt retains a nominal
predicate-disagreement flag. The participant’s physical and broader-reporting UNKNOWN is not a physical
self-judgment error. Both scopes are retained. Neither this disagreement nor the event PASS resolves the
inherited broader reporting contract. Earlier A/R dark NO_GO and FIFO FAIL remain historical failures;
O0 supplies no whole-product acceptance.
The one-second observations include dark-period brownouts near 8766.18 and 19587.64 s. The required active
intervals do not overlap the subsequent unobserved gaps. Successful O0 event coverage therefore does not
establish uninterrupted six-hour sensing or successful detection in every possible dark window. The next
waveform must be evaluated as a separate frozen task rather than inferred from the O0 final state. Physical
qualification remains UNKNOWN for all three arms.

Both failed stop receptions occur less than one second after the external confirmation instant. Nevertheless,
their encoded timestamps exceed the one-second error bound by 0.004 and 0.103 s. Sampling phase and the

specific to the frozen digital input and its timing, not a physical robustness guarantee.

retained. These differ from the original O0 candidate’s broader UNKNOWN: in the final failures the host
identifies a specific encoded event-time violation within the submitted nominal contract. Missing submissions
supply no service self-judgment at all.

6     Host cache incident and explicit protocol continuation
The later selection stage encounters a host representation error: historical observations.json is loaded
without its separately sealed trace.csv, while assessment requires the trajectory. Three selection attempts
raise KeyError:’trace’; a fourth attempt is truncated by the hold. These attempts are apparatus failures
or censoring, not negative G0/G1 method outcomes. The frozen hold preserves all four records and their
costs: 21 provider requests and USD 0.1318473 in settled upper-bound estimates [12].
The explicit amendment limits repair to reconstructing the already sealed historical trace and linking four
named one-off recovery attempts. Qualification passes exact-input historical assessment, preservation of
compressed raw and all five previously valid selection results, and rejection of missing trace, wrong trace
hash, wrong input and wrong world. These checks use existing responses with zero new provider requests or
simulations; they establish a cache decoding boundary, not method success [14].
Version 1.1 retains the original methods, tasks, scoring and request/time/dollar budgets, while preserving
every original attempt. Formal selection accounting uses the original proposal and two effective selection
attempts; incident overhead remains separately itemized and included in full arm/global pipeline cost. The
protocol retains 73 logical slots with a ceiling of 77 actual episodes after four named recoveries. Request,
summed episode-time and settled upper-bound cost ceilings remain 860, 54,400 s and USD 100. These are
ceilings, not observed final totals. The amendment preserves the five valid selection responses and twelve H
proposals rather than silently replacing them [13, 5]. The four named authorized recovery attempts all close
with PASS. They resolve the four canonical slots without rebuilding G/H or erasing the incident history [15].
Effective selection consequently has 30 cells, while its full accounting contains 34 actual attempts.

O0’s scope is one development input and an explicitly assumed digital apparatus. The results do not qualify
a photovoltaic device, PMIC transient model, vibration sensor, physical clock, NVM implementation or
RF receiver. They also do not erase prior A/R reporting failures. A/R NO_GO, dark FAIL, strict FIFO
FAIL and product NOT_ACCEPTED remain preserved. The independent O event gate does not cover
every inherited A/R metadata, reporting or dark-observation requirement. Physical qualification remains
UNKNOWN throughout.
The study establishes a finite separation: original O0 can be served by the same design as a strong fixed
control; a different waveform exposes encoded-time violations despite successful energy and event matching;
and procedural completion can fail through response truncation. It supplies neither evidence of an interface
advantage beyond rich-state nor a stable recursive-improvement mechanism. The unchanged H0’s own
successful 0.75 s submission and the single shared G1 formation constrain that interpretation.

8   Reproducible manuscript and evidence limits
render_phase4.py verifies the authoritative closed handoff and its finite source list, cross-checks actual and
final rows, frozen choices, accounting and per-event timestamp arithmetic, then generates this fragment. It
also preserves the original O0 participant explanation verbatim in phase4-binding.json. It runs no model,
scientific simulator or service evaluator. The source-preservation receipt records all six choices frozen before
final exposure, unchanged historical prefixes and preserved old UNKNOWN billing records [11].
The paper-final receipts bind both TeX sources, renderer, shared layout/logo, compiled PDF, fonts, extracted
text and actual page-by-page visual review. Build and rendering validate presentation and source binding;
they do not replicate the scientific batch, establish hardware acceptance or replace the coordinator’s registered
repository verification. No scientific code, task, result, old manuscript, shared style or logo is changed for this
paper.
