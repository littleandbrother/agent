                          Re-executing Scientific Design
                             after Method Changes

                                                Working draft

                              Public development manuscript, 20 October 2026




                                                  Abstract
     Changing a device, changing the method that designs it, and changing the procedure that improves
     that method invalidate different computations. We study these three interventions separately in
     an executable battery-free design apparatus. This development draft reports six deterministic
     invalidation controls and four fresh prompt-intervention attempts. Three delivered qualified
     computational designs; the fourth stopped at a frozen resource gate before validation. All 68
     model requests settled. The method update remains unpromoted. A separate, explicitly continued
     battery-free episode produced three device programs. A queue-rank change passes fresh-reception
     gates but violates the frozen oldest-first requirement; the twelve-hour dark service remains infeasible
     in the fixed ideal design space. The product is not accepted. No method advantage, recursive
     self-improvement, unseen-task transfer, hardware validation, or speedup is established.




1   Question and scope
A previously successful device trace can cease to describe the system after a legal change. Reusing that trace
may silently preserve obsolete control decisions. At the method level, a changed global design prompt may
alter the first observation, the candidate, and every later experiment. Evaluating the old device under that
prompt does not evaluate the changed method. Our question is which results remain valid after a typed
intervention, and how to confirm a proposed method change without charging a cached response as a new
design attempt.

2   Objects and validity contract
Let D contain device structure, circuits, firmware, storage, communication, and deployment. Let H contain
the design prompt, skills, available observations, context policy, experiment order, and organization. Let G
propose and select changes to H. Device runtime, the agent’s decision span, and method generations are
separate quantities. A record is reusable only when all consumed inputs, environment identities, numerical
settings, and output hashes remain applicable. An evaluation change may reuse raw physical output while
requiring a new verdict.
For a global prompt change H → H ′ , our conservative rule restarts at task entry with a new owner, initial
candidate, and context. Exact lower-level input responses may still be reused. A local continuation requires a
separate proof that its restored state is valid. A G comparison additionally fixes the starting H0 , development
distribution, selection protocol, and improvement budget.

4   F1b and early F4: real method re-execution
The frozen public task uses the already qualified piezoelectric, vendor PMIC, host-C controller, and digital
receiver apparatus. The power board is fixed; the agent must revise firmware and deliver six valid frames in
twenty seconds. The declared initial charge and current envelope remain assumptions. The task is an exposed
development derivative of historical T1, not a fresh holdout.
H0 requests one complete design attempt with execution, validation, decision, report, release, and close.
H1 adds a constraint and time-origin audit before a single justified revision, with version-bound evidence.
Both receive the same full task and sources, backend, tool definitions, limits, and initial native cache. The
prompt update was proposed by the research operator from earlier timing and tool-use failures; it is not an
autonomous improver. Two attempts per condition run in order H0 , H1 , H1 , H0 . Each allows at most 80
parent-plus-child requests, four candidate evaluations, and 1,200 seconds of decision wall time. Organization
is single-agent. No automatic resampling is allowed.
The protocol requires full execution to confirm an attempted method. Development retention requires both
H1 confirmations to deliver admissible results without an increased failure count. With two attempts per
condition, cost and quality differences are descriptive. A tie, failure, or no retained update is a valid result.

5     A/R: a battery-free entry to device and method changes
The A/R case places an indoor-light-powered temperature and history tag on a transport container. Its public
apparatus couples assumed photovoltaic port power, one capacitor, regulator thresholds, sensing and persistent
writes, program activity, and a digital receiving endpoint. Source power is specified in watts; illuminance is
metadata rather than a conversion to harvested power. All runs start with zero stored energy. The source,
ideal storage and write models, and storage-side energy costs are uncalibrated computational assumptions.

5.1    The retained reference and legal intervention
We re-read the sealed reference without rerunning an unchanged design. All 81 sealed files and 17 original
positive and negative conditions passed their original independent consumer. A phase-one consumer retains
those outcomes and adds explicit gate applicability and maximum inter-sample spacing. The six-hour profile
has 348 samples and its first fresh reception at 773 s. In the twelve-hour dark history profile, storage retains
all 170 records, including 168 temperature samples across the whole profile. Yet 648 of the 720 historical
dark-window bins contain no new sample. The recorded gap is delivered, but absolute time after reset remains
unknown; all 73 queued records miss the recovery deadline and one of two required temperature events is
missed.

The original gateway-recovery profile separates backlog completion from timeliness: its 60 queued records are

The frozen phase-one device space permits capacitance and the existing threshold domain, with maximum
voltage 3.3 V and all source and load assumptions fixed. The participant can change sample, ACK and retry
timing and supply a bounded numeric program that ranks pending records. This program observes sequence,
record kind, and age only when synchronized local time and the record timestamp support it. It cannot
observe true creation time after clock loss, future supply, or evaluator labels. Its expression is executed by
the queue consumer; submission alone cannot create an acceptance receipt. The frozen task also requires
the oldest unacknowledged record first. This ordering requirement must be checked separately from the rank
interface’s syntactic legality.

5.3   Conditional rank repair and the unresolved FIFO requirement
The independent consumer passes energy, actual activity/program replay, CRC and receiver checks for all
twelve case executions. Under the rank interface, all three candidates pass applicable computational service
gates in the cold, six-hour light/dark, and gateway recovery/deduplication profiles. In the gateway case
they retain 168 samples, acknowledge the 60-record backlog within 120 s, and accept no duplicate from two
repeated arrivals. Each of the 90 eligible new online samples is received without an extra simulation-step
delay. This changes the fresh-delay gate from the original FAIL to PASS under the rank policy.
This conditional timing repair is not compliant with the frozen FIFO requirement. Independent pending-record
replay finds strict ordering violations for all three candidates in both the history and gateway profiles. For the
submitted candidate, the gateway profile’s first witness occurs at 833 s: record 2 is selected while record 1 is
still the oldest unacknowledged record. The rank hook permits that choice, but the original task text does
not. The contract is unchanged and product acceptance remains NOT_ACCEPTED. A human must resolve this
semantic conflict before any revised ordering contract and its results can be accepted.
All three history profiles still fail continuous new sampling, dark new sampling, 120 s recovery upload and
temperature-event coverage; absolute time after reset remains UNKNOWN. Their whole-profile sample counts
are 106, 168 and 129, not counts of samples taken entirely within darkness. Faster retries reduce the remaining
late backlog while consuming energy that could support sensing. The selected candidate retains 170 records,
including 168 samples, and reports the gap, but all 73 recovery backlog records are late and one of two events

These disjoint necessary bounds establish a no-go only in the frozen ideal single-capacitor, fixed-sleep legal
space. They are not physical impossibility or a participant discovery. Optimistic necessary limits suggest
a cold deadline of at least 2,426.93 s (40.45 min), a dark duration of at most 32,072 s (8.91 h), or sleep
power below about 3.7084 µW. None is a sufficient design. Any changed service or apparatus needs a new

The episode totals seven SETTLED provider requests, including the two from the stopped segment. Their

6     Costs, exposure, and competing explanations
Report all parent and child requests, settled usage, reservations, failed tools, native dispatches, retries,
confirmations, cache construction, and unresolved UNKNOWN separately. Total cost is

                    C = Cproposal + Cselection + Cconfirmation + Cfailures + Ccache construction .

A valid cache hit does not remove the cost of building its entry. Paired attempts start from identical cache
manifests rather than warming later conditions with earlier outputs. Provider identity and usage availability
are dispatch gates. Preflight failures remain recorded, and cannot become successful attempts by silent
replacement. Mechanism exposure is shared with the interface study.
F1a supports a correctness boundary for this small control; it does not establish a general incremental
algorithm. F1b can establish that a prompt was actually changed and new design decisions executed.
Favorable development outcomes would still admit task familiarity, stochastic variation, and operator selection
as competing explanations. Later selection and final tasks must be frozen separately.

7   Related work and remaining experiments
Executable build-system decompositions provide a strong dependency-invalidation baseline [1]. Prompt
optimizers such as GEPA propose and evaluate prompt changes using execution feedback [2]. Our current
work does not establish superiority over either family. The planned study isolates physical-response reuse
from full method confirmation and retains negative cases. F2/F3 will explain decision spans and organizations
on a useful subset. F5 requires independent G0 /G1 lineages from a fixed H0 ; no such lineage experiment is
reported. F6 will follow the continuous A/R product after its service reference is qualified. Physical calibration
and hardware acceptance remain separate work.
