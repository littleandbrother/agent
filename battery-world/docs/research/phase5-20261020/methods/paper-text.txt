                     When a Design Method Changes:
                    Re-execution and Improver Lineages
                          in Battery-Free Design

                                                 Working draft

                             Complete experimental manuscript, 20 October 2026




                                                   Abstract
     A successful device does not by itself validate the method that designed it, and a new method does
     not establish an improved method proposer. We study these distinctions in a fixed executable
     battery-free apparatus. Our protocol separates device programs D, design procedures H, and
     procedures G that generate H; it requires fresh decisions after a global method change while
     allowing input-identical lower-level response reuse. An initially recovered model-authored method
     passes its device checks but fails independent promotion because initial cache parity is missing
     and its proposed necessary constraints exclude a passing baseline. A prospective comparison
     then yields 12/12 service passes for each of fixed H, ordinary reflection, and a typed procedure,
     using 64, 117, and 127 requests respectively. In a subsequent frozen transfer study, selected
     descendants yield 7/9, 6/9, and 8/9 passes for H0 , G0 , and G1 ; four token-censored attempts
     remain in the denominators. The three lineage pairs share one G1 formation, so these differences
     are descriptive. All 1,238 finite-campaign requests cost USD 10.0268025 at the frozen upper rate
     estimate. We retain the unchanged base method, provide saved-data reproduction, and preserve
     the A/R product’s dark-service and FIFO failures. No stable recursive improvement or physical
     qualification is established.




1   Introduction
An agent designing an energy-harvesting device may change a capacitor, rewrite firmware, revise its experiment
order, or change the instructions used to propose future revisions. These actions intervene on different objects.
Checking a new capacitor under old recorded controller actions can give the wrong physical response. Checking
an old device after rewriting the design prompt does not measure the new prompt’s design decisions. Selecting
a useful prompt does not show that the procedure which generated it has improved. A long history of tool
calls makes these distinctions harder to audit unless the experiment records both the intervention and the
evidence it invalidates.
Battery-free design makes the problem concrete. Harvested input, storage voltage, boot timing, event capture,
transmission, and later controller actions affect one another. A reset after an event was delivered can be
harmless for a single-event task, while a reset with pending volatile state can destroy service. For a history tag,
retaining already sampled records does not establish continued sensing in darkness. An apparently successful
upload can also conceal delayed fresh samples or a violation of the required queue order. These distinctions
require the actual service and temporal record, rather than an agent’s verbal diagnosis.
We use a fixed executable scientific apparatus to study device-level responses, fresh method execution, and

method-generating procedures. The apparatus and external evaluator are held outside the participant’s
authority. Tools expose source-bound facts; the participant can propose a legal artifact but cannot declare
acceptance or edit the service to make its artifact pass. We retain rejected calls, resource stops, unused
siblings, posthoc adaptations, and unresolved accounting alongside favorable results. The resulting experiment
can reject an update without treating rejection as a failure of the research process.
The empirical story has two successive tests of stronger claims. First, a method recovered from a failed live
proposal produces a successful device, but the independent confirmation gate rejects promotion. A later
prospective comparison matches initial caches and includes a strong fixed-method control. All three method
conditions pass every formal task/repetition; the two proposal conditions consume more requests. Second,
we compare ordinary reflection G0 with one actual model-authored proposer G1 , using paired descendant
lineages and frozen future inputs. The final counts differ, but the two digital derivatives saturate every arm
and the remaining service task contains both submitted failures and token censoring. These observations do
not establish a stable improvement process.
Our contribution is a source-bound experimental separation and its measured limits: a typed validity contract
for D/H/G changes; a complete negative method comparison after a failed initial confirmation; a conditional
improver-lineage study with all formation, sibling, selection, failure, and confirmation costs; and a portable
readback of the saved evidence. We do not claim a new general incremental simulator, superiority to a
state-of-the-art prompt optimizer, physical device acceptance, or an autonomous research system without
developer intervention.

2   Experimental objects and validity contract
Let W denote the fixed world, T the task and service contract, D the device program, and E the external
evaluator. The complete response Y = F (W, T, D) contains the coupled physical and controller trajectory,
and E(T, D, Y ) produces the computational service result. A design procedure H chooses observations, orders
experiments, and produces D from a fresh task entry. A proposer G reads an admitted development history
and generates candidate H artifacts for a registered selection procedure. The device’s runtime, an agent’s
decision span, and successive method generations are different experimental axes.

For a changed D, a reusable record must match all consumed inputs, apparatus identities, numerical settings,
and output bindings. The old trajectory is an explicit negative control when a coupled input changes.
Dependency and boundary/scope invalidation may conservatively expand to the complete feedback response.
An exact-input cache can reuse a qualified response; building that cache still has a cost. An evaluator-only
change may reuse the same raw response while requiring a new verdict. It cannot silently replace the previous
verdict.
For a global H → H ′ change, our conservative policy restarts decisions at task entry with a new owner and
empty decision context. It does not replay the old agent’s reasoning as evidence for H ′ . Qualified lower-level
responses may still be reused when their consumed inputs match. A smaller restored decision suffix would
need its own state-validity proof; no such advantage is reported here. A G comparison additionally fixes H0 ,
the allowed development history, selection tasks, future-input exclusion, and total improvement budget. Every
candidate branch, including an unselected sibling, remains part of that cost.
Promotion is a separate decision from schema eligibility and service success. The method artifact records a
hypothesis, falsifier, typed change and scope. The external registry checks source, tool, model configuration,

evaluator, initial cache, fresh-context and cost identities. A structural pass admits an artifact for testing;
it does not validate the artifact’s scientific claims. The default method changes only under the registered
retention rule.

3     Apparatus, tasks, and measurements
The principal apparatus is an ideal digital battery-free model with piecewise source input, finite storage,
conversion and sleep costs, boot and loaded-voltage thresholds, event capture, and a receiving endpoint. In
the small BF tasks, legal device edits are capacitance and the earliest allowed transmit time. Event, source,
initial state, all other device fields and evaluator stay fixed. The service requires the specified application
frame by its deadline without duplicate acceptance. Independent consumers check energy, receiver behavior,
transient voltage and deduplication. Passing these computations is not physical calibration or an official
benchmark result.
The prospective method comparison uses four fixed task IDs: fast-pulse BF, volatile-reset BF, latched-reset
BF, and a synthetic C-Lite single-query DC derivative. They exercise distinct mechanisms within a small
declared model, not four independent hardware families. The C-Lite case does not qualify RF, nonce security,
multiple-query operation, or a real radio deployment. The formal task list is finite; repeated sampling on a
task is not an additional task.
The continuous A/R case concerns an indoor-light-powered temperature/history tag. It couples photovoltaic
port power, a capacitor, regulator thresholds, sensing, persistent writes, record ranking, transmission and a
digital gateway. Source power is specified in watts; illuminance is descriptive metadata. The legal apparatus
starts with no stored energy and has an uncalibrated source, storage and write model. It provides an
engineering counterexample to broad completion claims, not a physical demonstration. Section 7 preserves
that case’s separate service and ordering requirements.
We measure independently evaluated service, participant service judgment, ordered capture–reset–delivery
events, admitted response identities, provider requests, token-based charge estimates, summed episode wall
time, failed actions, resource censoring, native dispatch and new scientific execution. Requests, logical design
checks, cached response files and physical experiments are not interchangeable counts. Public feedback is
available to the participant; the external final verdict remains separate. Developer assistance, model-weight
identity and physical qualification have their own boundaries.

4     From recorded failures to an independently rejected method

4.1    Response controls and an initial prompt probe
Six deterministic public controls first compare frozen-trajectory replay, dependency invalidation, boundary
invalidation, full response execution and exact-input response caching. The apparatus has arrivals, finite
capacity, a threshold controller and action-dependent consumption. The unchanged case is a positive reuse
control. Changing energy, load, threshold, capacity or another consumed input makes the frozen trajectory
disagree with the full response in the five changed cases. Dependency, boundary and exact-input strategies
match full response on all six. In coupled cases the two invalidation strategies expand to full response; this is
a correctness qualification with no demonstrated physical-solver speedup.
An initial public firmware task then tests actual method re-execution, rather than replay. The power board
is fixed and the participant revises firmware to deliver six valid frames in twenty seconds. The operator-
authored H1 adds a constraint and time-origin audit to the base instruction. Four fresh attempts run in
order H0 , H1 , H1 , H0 with the same initial artifacts and native-cache boundary. Each permits at most 80
parent-plus-child requests, four candidate evaluations, and 1,200 seconds. Both H1 attempts and the first H0
attempt deliver qualified computational designs. The final H0 attempt stops at the frozen disk-headroom
gate before validation. It remains a censored attempt, not a replacement opportunity. All 68 requests settle,
at USD 1.1429220 upper. The prompt was supplied by the operator, not an autonomous G, and the default
method remains unpromoted.

4.2    A real model-authored method, recovered posthoc
In the next method episode, the model is asked to propose a repeatable H. The live proposal instead encounters
a wrongly exposed host action and a candidate-slot contract failure: a device-check payload consumes a
method slot. The episode uses nine requests and ends incomplete with no successful method submission. A
posthoc host adapter recovers the actual model-authored method from the retained rejected call, adding no
paid proposal or new model decision. The recovered artifact is eligible for experimental re-execution; the
original live episode remains a failure.
The proposed method acquires sources, constructs an energy/timing ledger, tracks volatile event loss, and
checks a design. It also states task-specific exclusions: a necessary 7.4–14 µF band, rejection of waiting across
the dark gap or using a late transmit floor, and a recommended 10 µF / 2 ms candidate. These statements
are scientific claims, not consequences of a valid method schema. The independent test must retain both the
proposed falsifier and any over-restrictive rule.
Fresh H0 and H ′ entries each begin with zero decision messages, distinct owners, the same task and sources,
and caps of two candidates, 24 requests and 1,200 seconds. Each checks and submits one candidate. The
base method chooses 22 µF / 45 ms; the revised method chooses 9 µF / 3.7 ms, rather than copying the
recommended values. Both pass the full frozen computational service. First reception is 46.9 ms and 5.6 ms
respectively. These are fresh device decisions; external readback reuses their exact-input complete responses.

4.3    Independent confirmation rejects promotion
Two observations prevent a method-benefit claim. First, the physical cache evolved between entries and its
initial snapshots were not frozen equally. The registry explicitly rejects initial-cache parity. Input-correct
reuse of an individual response does not establish equal starting cache exposure. Second, the successful 22 µF
/ 45 ms baseline contradicts the proposed necessary band and rejection of a late floor. It does not test the
separate specific 22 µF / 2 ms edge case or the recommended device, and does not refute the distinct claim
that transmission fits within one contiguous segment. A successful revised device cannot validate every rule of
its method.
The event-order audit also corrects a tempting mechanism interpretation. The baseline has no reset before
delivery or deadline. The revised design delivers at 5.6 ms and resets at 12.1 ms, after completion. Both keep
the event latch volatile and have zero pre-delivery resets. A legacy success-and-any-reset label marks the
revised design as reset retention, but these traces do not show pending state crossing reset. The checker and
legacy labels remain intact; their interpretation follows capture–reset–delivery order.
The external decision is to reject promotion and retain the experimental artifact. Proposal plus revised design
costs 14 requests and USD 0.0729432, versus the baseline’s five requests and USD 0.0393375. Across the four
episodes in this finite batch, including an independent incomplete interface-consumer attempt, all 25 requests
settle at USD 0.1766922 upper. Seven recorded new local computational cases include the qualification grid
and consumer/design work; the editorial readback adds none. This comparison motivates a prospective
protocol with matched cache exposure; it is not retroactively repaired by it.

5     Prospective comparison: a strong baseline saturates the tasks

5.1    Conditions, parity, and frozen sampling
The next protocol compares three method conditions under complete-raw information: fixed H0 , ordinary
reflection, and a typed dependency/evidence-scope procedure. Complete-raw supplies all declared sources
and the fixed model definition; the host retains full raw traces for audit. Ordinary reflection reviews the
provided observations and proposes a method or no update. The typed procedure additionally requests a
D/H distinction, source-dependent evidence scope, event ordering, and separation of necessary constraints
from sufficient heuristics. It supplies no winning device values. Both proposing conditions must produce
actual model-authored hypothesis, falsifier and method text.



                                                       4
Each task/condition/repetition group has the same total cap of 24 requests, 1,200 seconds and two distinct D
candidates. The proposal subcap of eight requests and 400 seconds is included, leaving less for fresh design
when it is used. Acquisition, failed actions, proposals, no-update decisions, fresh design and confirmation are
charged. Equal ceilings do not imply equal realized spending. A no-update retains its cost and uses the base
method for an admitted fresh design.
Every arm starts from the same immutable cache manifest of 12 prior responses plus one registered derived
baseline. Job overlays are isolated. Later exact-input deduplication does not expose another arm’s output
before its query or increase the logical two-design allowance. All 28 matched proposal groups, including pilots,
pass the recorded information, tool, model-alias, cache and total-budget parity checks. These are eligibility
checks, not evidence of benefit.
Two pilot repetitions reverse condition order. The registered rule selects two formal repetitions only if service
and design signatures are stable in every matched arm; otherwise it selects three. The pilot signatures are not
stable, so three repetitions are frozen once before formal execution. No original failure is replaced and no
outcome-dependent extension follows that freeze. The complete eight-condition matrix has 16 pilot and 96
formal groups, comprising 140 episodes and 771 requests. The method subset uses six pilot and 36 formal
groups within that matrix. Its fixed-method cell is shared with the interface study and counted once.

5.3   Correct recomputation without local savings
The response audit annotates 107 existing admitted group responses; five groups have no admitted final
response. Of those 107, 91 change D and 16 are unchanged controls. A frozen old trajectory is ineligible after
a changed coupled input. Dependency, scope and full-feedback strategies share one actual complete-response
execution key in every admitted record. The first two conservatively expand to full response, demonstrating
no valid smaller suffix or speed advantage. For a global H change, all valid policies require fresh task-entry
decisions. This deterministic audit adds zero scientific execution and is not four new paid strategy arms.

6     A model-authored improver and frozen descendant transfer

6.1    From original experience to G1
The next study holds the base H0 unchanged and compares ordinary reflection G0 with an actual model-
authored proposal procedure G1 . One formation episode reads an admitted packet drawn from four retained
public method/design episodes. Both proposer conditions receive the same mechanical model-history packet:
75,955 bytes with a recorded hash. The larger 257,661-byte host history is not the model packet. The
original-service O0 task and every final input are excluded from formation and selection. The final design
entries receive their own current frozen task facts, including the waveform; those facts are not secret from the
executing design agent.
The single G1 formation uses three requests, USD 0.0269157 upper and 38.72 summed seconds. Its 4,599-
character procedure asks for a capability ledger, typed symbolic methods, per-world latch/source branching,
service-first triage, retrospective rehearsal, an explicit falsifier, cost/error retention and a legal proposal
channel. It permits no update. It changes procedural text, not model weights. Eligibility and a source audit do
not establish that every generated rule is scientifically valid. In particular, its broad instruction to treat events
after the last transmission as benign can fail for ongoing or multiple-event service, and assumed source/channel
fields may be unavailable. We retain these defects without hand-editing the generated procedure.
For each of three paired lineages, G0 and G1 independently generate two H siblings starting from H0 , giving
twelve actual proposals. There is one shared G1 formation, not three independent training replications. Every
proposal is scope-eligible, with scientific validity not established at submission. None is repaired or excluded
by hand. Six G0 proposals use 20 requests and USD 0.1832424; six G1 proposals use 23 and USD 0.2658975.
The combined formation/proposal stage costs 46 requests and USD 0.4760556.
Fifteen recorded unknown-public-source tool failures occur across five proposal episodes, including one G0
episode and four G1 episodes. These are source access failures, not unresolved billing or native executions.
They remain in traces and costs. They do not justify treating all model descriptions of “schema rejection” as
confirmed schema failures.

7     A/R engineering consequence: a conditional repair still fails
The A/R tag provides a separate continuous device-level case. Its retained reference has 348 samples on
a six-hour profile and first fresh reception at 773 s. In a twelve-hour dark history profile it retains all 170
records, including 168 whole-profile temperature samples, yet 648 of 720 historical dark-window bins contain
no new sample. All 73 backlog records miss the recovery deadline and one of two required temperature events
is missed. In gateway recovery, all 60 queued records are acknowledged within 82 s and duplicate arrivals
cause no duplicate acceptance, but one fresh sample waits 30 s. Retention, new sensing, eventual upload and
fresh delay are distinct gates.

originally unresolved request later settles, while its original UNKNOWN snapshot and failure remain saved.

9    Discussion and related work
The experiment distinguishes evidence validity from improvement. Conservative invalidation can be correct
while saving no coupled computation. A recovered method can produce a valid device while failing a fair-
comparison gate. A prospective method can pass every task without adding a success to the strong baseline.
A formed improver can yield favorable descendant counts without establishing repeated self-improvement.
These are different claims, each with its own denominator and falsifier.
Build-system work provides an executable decomposition of dependency and rebuilding policies [1]. That is a
strong conceptual comparison for evidence invalidation. Our coupled examples demonstrate a conservative
full-response boundary; they do not outperform such systems or establish a general incremental algorithm.
GEPA uses reflected execution histories to propose, test and combine prompt updates [2]. Our ordinary
reflection control is a simple registered procedure, not a faithful GEPA implementation, and no direct GEPA
comparison is claimed. The present evidence supports an audit of a finite apparatus and of its negative results,
rather than a broad optimizer ranking.
DSPy expresses LM pipelines through declarative modules and optimizes them against a chosen metric [3].
STOP improves an LM-calling improver program, distinguishing scaffold revisions from changes to LM
weights [4]. Thus, optimizing procedures and separating improvement levels predate our D/H/G notation.
Our study instead fixes battery-free service semantics and audits fresh decisions, exposure, censoring and
complete selection costs. Neither DSPy nor STOP is directly evaluated here. For intermittent execution,
Alpaca uses task-based execution and privatized state updates to support safe restart [5]. We do not implement
or outperform that memory mechanism: our cross-reset witness is an inherited ideal latch, and retained state
alone does not supply missing sensing energy.
The strongest method control already succeeds on every formal task. This ceiling limits sensitivity to an
improved procedure. The later waveform task creates variation but simultaneously exposes long reasoning-
output censoring. The data cannot identify how much of a descendant difference comes from specific method
clauses, text length, stochastic decisions or the chosen formation history. No component ablation, inferential
significance test or population-level success-rate estimate is justified by this task count.
The next evidential step for an improvement claim would require independently formed G1 procedures,
prospectively frozen new tasks, matched exposure and retention rules, and a budget covering all attempts. It
would also need to separate source acquisition, service judgment and device success. A shorter or cheaper final
design alone would remain insufficient. Those experiments are not performed in this delivery. The observed
decision is to retain H0 , the original failures, and the limited scope of every computational PASS.
