When a Design Method Changes: Re-execution and Improver Lineages in Battery-Free Design Working draft Complete experimental manuscript, 20 October 2026 Abstract A successful device does not by itself validate the method that designed it, and a new method does not establish an improved method proposer. We study these distinctions in a fixed executable battery-free apparatus. Our protocol separates device programs D, design procedures H, and procedures G that generate H; it requires fresh decisions after a global method change while allowing input-identical lower-level response reuse. An initially recovered model-authored method passes its device checks but fails independent promotion because initial cache parity is missing and its proposed necessary constraints exclude a passing baseline. A prospective comparison then yields 12/12 service passes for each of fixed H, ordinary reflection, and a typed procedure, using 64, 117, and 127 requests respectively. In a subsequent frozen transfer study, selected descendants yield 7/9, 6/9, and 8/9 passes for H 0 , G 0 , and G 1 ; four token-censored attempts remain in the denominators. The three lineage pairs share one G 1 formation, so these differences are descriptive. All 1,238 finite-campaign requests cost USD 10.0268025 at the frozen upper rate estimate. We retain the unchanged base method, provide saved-data reproduction, and preserve the A/R product’s dark-service and FIFO failures. No stable recursive improvement or physical qualification is established. 1 Introduction An agent designing an energy-harvesting device may change a capacitor, rewrite firmware, revise its experiment order, or change the instructions used to propose future revisions. These actions intervene on different objects. Checking a new capacitor under old recorded controller actions can give the wrong physical response. Checking an old device after rewriting the design prompt does not measure the new prompt’s design decisions. Selecting a useful prompt does not show that the procedure which generated it has improved. A long history of tool calls makes these distinctions harder to audit unless the experiment records both the intervention and the evidence it invalidates. Battery-free design makes the problem concrete. Harvested input, storage voltage, boot timing, event capture, transmission, and later controller actions affect one another. A reset after an event was delivered can be harmless for a single-event task, while a reset with pending volatile state can destroy service. For a history tag, retaining already sampled records does not establish continued sensing in darkness. An apparently successful upload can also conceal delayed fresh samples or a violation of the required queue order. These distinctions require the actual service and temporal record, rather than an agent’s verbal diagnosis. We use a fixed executable scientific apparatus to study device-level responses, fresh method execution, and 1 method-generating procedures. The apparatus and external evaluator are held outside the participant’s authority. Tools expose source-bound facts; the participant can propose a legal artifact but cannot declare acceptance or edit the service to make its artifact pass. We retain rejected calls, resource stops, unused siblings, posthoc adaptations, and unresolved accounting alongside favorable results. The resulting experiment can reject an update without treating rejection as a failure of the research process. The empirical story has two successive tests of stronger claims. First, a method recovered from a failed live proposal produces a successful device, but the independent confirmation gate rejects promotion. A later prospective comparison matches initial caches and includes a strong fixed-method control. All three method conditions pass every formal task/repetition; the two proposal conditions consume more requests. Second, we compare ordinary reflection G 0 with one actual model-authored proposer G 1 , using paired descendant lineages and frozen future inputs. The final counts differ, but the two digital derivatives saturate every arm and the remaining service task contains both submitted failures and token censoring. These observations do not establish a stable improvement process. Our contribution is a source-bound experimental separation and its measured limits: a typed validity contract for D/H/G changes; a complete negative method comparison after a failed initial confirmation; a conditional improver-lineage study with all formation, sibling, selection, failure, and confirmation costs; and a portable readback of the saved evidence. We do not claim a new general incremental simulator, superiority to a state-of-the-art prompt optimizer, physical device acceptance, or an autonomous research system without developer intervention. 2 Experimental objects and validity contract Let W denote the fixed world, T the task and service contract, D the device program, and E the external evaluator. The default method changes only under the registered retention rule. 3 Apparatus, tasks, and measurements The principal apparatus is an ideal digital battery-free model with piecewise source input, finite storage, conversion and sleep costs, boot and loaded-voltage thresholds, event capture, and a receiving endpoint. In the small BF tasks, legal device edits are capacitance and the earliest allowed transmit time. Event, source, initial state, all other device fields and evaluator stay fixed. The service requires the specified application frame by its deadline without duplicate acceptance. Independent consumers check energy, receiver behavior, transient voltage and deduplication. Passing these computations is not physical calibration or an official benchmark result. The prospective method comparison uses four fixed task IDs: fast-pulse BF, volatile-reset BF, latched-reset BF, and a synthetic C-Lite single-query DC derivative. They exercise distinct mechanisms within a small declared model, not four independent hardware families. The C-Lite case does not qualify RF, nonce security, multiple-query operation, or a real radio deployment. The formal task list is finite; repeated sampling on a task is not an additional task. The continuous A/R case concerns an indoor-light-powered temperature/history tag. It couples photovoltaic port power, a capacitor, regulator thresholds, sensing, persistent writes, record ranking, transmission and a digital gateway. Source power is specified in watts; illuminance is descriptive metadata. The legal apparatus starts with no stored energy and has an uncalibrated source, storage and write model. It provides an engineering counterexample to broad completion claims, not a physical demonstration. Section 7 preserves that case’s separate service and ordering requirements. We measure independently evaluated service, participant service judgment, ordered capture–reset–delivery events, admitted response identities, provider requests, token-based charge estimates, summed episode wall time, failed actions, resource censoring, native dispatch and new scientific execution. Requests, logical design checks, cached response files and physical experiments are not interchangeable counts. These are fresh device decisions; external readback reuses their exact-input complete responses. 4.3 Independent confirmation rejects promotion Two observations prevent a method-benefit claim. First, the physical cache evolved between entries and its initial snapshots were not frozen equally. The registry explicitly rejects initial-cache parity. Input-correct reuse of an individual response does not establish equal starting cache exposure. Second, the successful 22 µF / 45 ms baseline contradicts the proposed necessary band and rejection of a late floor. It does not test the separate specific 22 µF / 2 ms edge case or the recommended device, and does not refute the distinct claim that transmission fits within one contiguous segment. A successful revised device cannot validate every rule of its method. The event-order audit also corrects a tempting mechanism interpretation. The baseline has no reset before delivery or deadline. The revised design delivers at 5.6 ms and resets at 12.1 ms, after completion. Both keep the event latch volatile and have zero pre-delivery resets. A legacy success-and-any-reset label marks the revised design as reset retention, but these traces do not show pending state crossing reset. The checker and legacy labels remain intact; their interpretation follows capture–reset–delivery order. The external decision is to reject promotion and retain the experimental artifact. Proposal plus revised design costs 14 requests and USD 0.0729432, versus the baseline’s five requests and USD 0.0393375. Across the four episodes in this finite batch, including an independent incomplete interface-consumer attempt, all 25 requests settle at USD 0.1766922 upper. Seven recorded new local computational cases include the qualification grid and consumer/design work; the editorial readback adds none. A passing CI, package hash or PDF build cannot substitute for scientific or product qualification. 9 Discussion and related work The experiment distinguishes evidence validity from improvement. Conservative invalidation can be correct while saving no coupled computation. A recovered method can produce a valid device while failing a fair- comparison gate. A prospective method can pass every task without adding a success to the strong baseline. A formed improver can yield favorable descendant counts without establishing repeated self-improvement. These are different claims, each with its own denominator and falsifier. Build-system work provides an executable decomposition of dependency and rebuilding policies [1]. That is a strong conceptual comparison for evidence invalidation. Our coupled examples demonstrate a conservative full-response boundary; they do not outperform such systems or establish a general incremental algorithm. GEPA uses reflected execution histories to propose, test and combine prompt updates [2]. Our ordinary reflection control is a simple registered procedure, not a faithful GEPA implementation, and no direct GEPA comparison is claimed. The present evidence supports an audit of a finite apparatus and of its negative results, rather than a broad optimizer ranking. The strongest method control already succeeds on every formal task. This ceiling limits sensitivity to an improved procedure. The later waveform task creates variation but simultaneously exposes long reasoning- output censoring. The data cannot identify how much of a descendant difference comes from specific method clauses, text length, stochastic decisions or the chosen formation history. No component ablation, inferential significance test or population-level success-rate estimate is justified by this task count. The next evidential step for an improvement claim would require independently formed G 1 procedures, prospectively frozen new tasks, matched exposure and retention rules, and a budget covering all attempts. It would also need to separate source acquisition, service judgment and device success. A shorter or cheaper final design alone would remain insufficient.
